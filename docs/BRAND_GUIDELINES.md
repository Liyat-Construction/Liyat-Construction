# Liyat Construction — Brand Guidelines

> **Version 1.1 · 2026-10-20 · Draft for client approval**
> Prepared by Digital Aksumite. Becomes final when Wondwossen (CEO) approves it.
> See the [change log](#13-change-log) for what changed from v1.0 and why.

This guide defines how Liyat Construction looks, sounds and presents itself — on
screen, on paper and on site. The website is its first full implementation; the
code-level translation lives in [`DESIGN_SYSTEM.md`](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md).

Where a situation isn't covered here, choose the simpler option and the one
closest to the rest of the brand.

---

## 1. Who we are

Liyat Construction develops groundwater. We take a water project from
hydrogeological survey through drilling, well construction and testing to pump
installation, and we hand over sources built to last.

Our clients are public bodies, development agencies, embassies, NGOs, farms,
factories and institutions. They need water that will still be there in ten
years, and they choose a contractor on proof: the rig, the drilling log, the
pump test, the finished well.

## 2. Character

| We are                                                        | We are not           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **Precise** — specific about depths, yields and timelines     | Vague or salesy      |
| **Dependable** — we do what we say and show the record        | Over-promising       |
| **Technically sure** — we know the ground and the equipment   | Jargon-heavy or cold |
| **Responsible** — careful with water, land, people and safety | Preachy about it     |
| **Approachable** — easy to call, easy to understand           | Casual or careless   |

**The brand should feel like a well-run site: orderly, safe and quietly confident.**
For the website this means: an engineering document that happens to be a
website. White, aligned, measured. Real site photographs carry the emotion;
numbers carry the persuasion; green marks every action.

## 3. Logo

The letters **L** and **C** in Liyat Green, divided by a vertical bar, with a
blue water drop held inside the C. The letters stand for the company; the drop
stands for the work.

### Versions

| Version         | Use                                                               |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Full color      | On white or Stone backgrounds. Website header.                    |
| All white       | On Ink, Liyat Green, or a calm dark area of a photograph. Footer. |
| Mark (C + drop) | Below minimum size; favicon; social profile picture.              |

### Rules

- **Clear space:** at least three times the width of the vertical bar on every side.
- **Minimum size:** 80 px wide on screen (20 mm in print). Smaller → use the mark.
- **Backgrounds:** never on a busy image or a mid-tone color where the green or drop gets lost.
- **Never:** stretch, squash, tilt, rotate; change or swap colors; add shadows,
  outlines, glows or 3D; retype the letters; move, resize or detach the drop.
- **Master files:** SVG for the web, PDF for print, PNG exports for everyday use.
  The current source is a low-resolution JPG; a vector master is required (C-01).

## 4. Color

Liyat runs on **one brand color, one dark neutral and white**, with two
supporting tones and one meaningful accent. No other colors are used.

| Token           | HEX       | Role                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Liyat Green** | `#156635` | The brand. Buttons, links, active states, the signature rule, key highlights, logo.                                                                                       |
| Green Dark      | `#0F4F28` | Hover and pressed state of green elements only.                                                                                                                           |
| Green Tint      | `#E7F0EA` | Callout boxes, table rows, selected states. (The brochure's soft sage, made legal.)                                                                                       |
| Green Light     | `#A9DDBA` | Lines on dark: the signature rule and current-page underline. Also footer column headings and legal-page eyebrows on plain Ink only (D-90, D-91); never text over photos. |
| **Ink**         | `#1D2521` | Body text, headings, footer, dark sections. A charcoal with a green undertone.                                                                                            |
| Ink Muted       | `#56615B` | Secondary text, captions, metadata, form borders.                                                                                                                         |
| **Paper**       | `#F8F7F3` | Main background of every page. A warm off-white, so white cards stand out from it.                                                                                        |
| **White**       | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, form fields, header; text on dark backgrounds.                                                                                                                     |
| Stone           | `#EEEBE3` | Fills only (image placeholders, ground in diagrams); not a page section. Warm neutral taken from the earth in our photographs.                                            |
| Line            | `#DCDAD3` | Hairline dividers and card borders (decorative only — never the only cue).                                                                                                |
| **Water Blue**  | `#0B5CA0` | **Water only**: yields, water levels, flow figures, the water in technical drawings.                                                                                      |
| Alert Red       | `#B42318` | Form errors only. Functional, never decorative.                                                                                                                           |

### Why these colors

- **Green leads** because it is the only brand equity Liyat already owns: logo,
  brochure, vehicles and uniforms. Recognition across every touchpoint beats any
  new palette. `#156635` is the green used by Liyat's own designed brochure; it is
  more refined than the flat green in the logo JPG (`#006000`) and reaches 7 : 1
  contrast on white.
- **Blue means water.** It is taken from the drop in the logo and used exactly
  as the drop is used: small, and only where water is the subject. Color that
  carries meaning instead of decoration.
- **Stone brings warmth** that pure white and green lack, and ties the page to
  the dust, gravel and earth in Liyat's photographs.
- **No gold, no navy.** Gold fails contrast (2.6 : 1), competes with the yellow
  machinery in our photographs, and pushes the tone toward luxury. Navy sits too
  close in depth to green, so the two compete.

### Balance

Screens and documents: roughly **70% Paper/White/Stone, 18% Ink, 10% Green, 2% Water Blue**.
On screen the page is Paper and the cards on it are White: a quiet step in
brightness that separates them without shadows. Print stays on white paper.
Vehicles, uniforms and signs carry far more green.

### Approved combinations (contrast measured, WCAG 2.2)

| Text / element on background        | Ratio           | Use                    |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------- | ---------------------- |
| Ink on White / Paper                | 15.7 / 14.6     | Body text              |
| Ink on Stone                        | 13.2            | Text on Stone fills    |
| Ink Muted on White / Paper          | 6.5 / 6.0       | Secondary text         |
| Ink Muted on Stone                  | 5.4             | Secondary text         |
| Green on White / Paper              | 7.0 / 6.6       | Links, labels, figures |
| Green on Stone / Green Tint         | 5.9 / 6.1       | Links and labels       |
| White on Green                      | 7.0             | Buttons, CTA band      |
| White on Green Dark                 | 9.7             | Button hover           |
| White on Ink                        | 15.7            | Footer, dark sections  |
| Water Blue on White / Paper / Stone | 6.9 / 6.4 / 5.8 | Water figures          |
| Alert Red on White / Paper          | 6.6 / 6.1       | Form errors            |

### Forbidden combinations

- **Green on Ink** (2.2 : 1) — unreadable. On Ink, links and actions are white.
- Green on Water Blue or Water Blue on Green — they vibrate and lose meaning.
- Any color text on a photograph without a scrim.
- Tints as text colors. Tints are fills; text stays in full color.

## 5. Typography

| Typeface    | Used for                                                     | Weights                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| **Manrope** | Titles, headings, short statements, large figures            | SemiBold 600, Bold 700                |
| **Inter**   | Body text, tables, forms, captions, labels, technical detail | Regular 400, Medium 500, SemiBold 600 |
| Arial       | Fallback only — never mixed with the main fonts in one file  | —                                     |

- Headings in **sentence case** ("Selected projects", not "Selected Projects").
- Body text left-aligned, never justified, line height 1.6 on screen.
- **Figures use tabular numerals** so depths, yields and dates align like a data sheet.
- Bold marks the one phrase a reader must not miss, and nothing else.
- The web type scale is defined once in [`DESIGN_SYSTEM.md` §2.2](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md#22-typography).

## 6. Photography

Our own site photographs are the most persuasive thing the brand has. A rig at
work or clean water rising from a new well says more than any headline. **Liyat
materials use real Liyat photographs. Stock images never represent our work.**

### What we photograph — every project, start to finish

1. The site before work begins
2. The rig set up and drilling, with the Liyat name in view
3. The crew at work in full protective equipment
4. Casing, gravel pack and installation
5. The pump test and water at the surface
6. The finished well and the handover

Each photograph is filed with project name, location and date.

### How we photograph

Daylight, sun behind the camera. A wide shot for context and a close shot for
detail at each stage. Mostly landscape; a few portrait shots for social media.
Tidy frame: move loose tools, bottles and rubbish out of shot.

### Safety and dignity

A photograph that shows unsafe work is never published. People photographed up
close give permission first; anyone featured prominently gives written consent.
Community members — children above all — are shown with dignity, as people
using their water, never as objects of charity.

### Editing

Crop, straighten, correct brightness and color so photographs sit well together.
**Nothing is added, removed or altered that changes what happened on site.**

### On the website

- The home hero is one photograph filling the first screen: the Liyat rig fleet on site in the
  Afar region, under a flat Ink scrim and a soft Ink fade at the top for the menu (D-81). A photo
  with equipment and water at the surface together remains the ideal once originals exist (C-18).
- Text over a photograph sits on a flat Ink scrim (see §7); the photograph keeps
  its natural color — never a green or blue color wash.
- Technical specs for sizes and formats: [`ENGINEERING.md` §5](ENGINEERING.md#5-images).

## 7. Layout and graphic elements

A Liyat layout looks like a good engineering drawing: aligned, measured and
uncluttered. Space is part of the design; every element has a job.

- **Grid.** Everything aligns to a 12-column grid. Margins are generous.
- **The rule.** Liyat Green marks the header's bottom edge and the start of the dark closing block. No short lines above headings (D-82). Between every page's Paper content sections (D-102), a full-width 1 px Line-neutral hairline at 60% opacity quietly separates sections; no extra divider against the hero or closing block.
- **Icons.** Simple line icons (1.5 px stroke) in one color, used only where they
  help someone find or understand something.
- **Charts and diagrams.** Water Blue for water, Liyat Green for structure and
  Liyat elements, Ink for labels and axes. No 3D, no shadows.
- **Technical drawings** — well cross-sections, borehole logs, site maps — are
  part of the brand. The website's signature visual is a **well cross-section**
  linking each service to the part of the well it builds.
- **Corners** are crisp: small radii (6–8 px), never pill-shaped buttons.
- **Shadows** are almost absent: borders and space separate things.
- **Scrims.** A flat Ink overlay (≤ 70% opacity) over a photograph is allowed for
  text legibility. That is function, not decoration.

- **The drawing grid.** On plain Ink surfaces only (the closing section, page headers
  without a photo): faint lines like a site plan, with a small survey mark (+) at some
  crossings. It is never laid over a photograph; the photograph is the texture there.

**Never:** the drop repeated as a pattern, bullet or background; waves;
decorative gradients (the hero's top fade is functional: it keeps the menu readable, D-81); glows;
shine effects; heavy shadows; clip art.

## 8. Voice and messaging

We write the way an experienced engineer talks to a client: plainly,
specifically and without exaggeration. Facts persuade; adjectives don't. If a
sentence could appear in any company's brochure, it gets rewritten.

| Generic                                           | Liyat                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A leading provider of world-class water solutions | A specific fact: how many wells, how deep, for which client           |
| State-of-the-art equipment                        | The rig by name and the depth it is rated to                          |
| Guaranteed success                                | We survey before we drill and test every well before handover.        |
| Eco-friendly operations                           | The actual practice, e.g. how drilling fluid and cuttings are handled |

**What we talk about:** Expertise (reading the ground) · Execution (equipment,
crews, methods) · Evidence (projects, test results, clients) · Reliability
(clear communication, honest timelines) · Responsibility (safe sites, careful
use of water and land).

**Website microcopy:** buttons are verbs in sentence case — "Contact us",
"Explore projects", "Request a site survey". Never "Get started", "Learn more",
"Click here".

### Claims

Every statement about experience, capacity, certification, partnerships or
environmental practice is backed by a document we can produce on request: a
contract, a completion certificate, a test report or a licence. **What we
cannot show, we do not say.** Client names appear only with permission.

## 9. Service names

Each service has one name, used identically on the website, in proposals and on
invoices. They are listed in project-lifecycle order.

1. Hydrogeological & Geophysical Survey
2. Borehole Drilling
3. Pumping Tests
4. Borehole Rehabilitation
5. Pump Supply & Installation (submersible and hand pumps, with the electromechanical works)
6. Turnkey Water Projects

Source: the client's own lists (brochure, the services list and Wondwossen's message of
2026-10-22; D-65). Mining, core drilling, water-quality testing and excavation shoring
are **not** listed until the client confirms them with a project and the equipment (C-09, C-32).

## 12. Looking after the brand

All brand material lives in one shared brand folder, managed by the CEO,
Wondwossen: logo files, this palette (and Pantone matches for print), font
links, templates, selected photographs by project, and the approved texts.

Wondwossen signs off anything new that carries the logo before it is printed or
published. The logo, colors and typefaces change only by a management decision;
the rest of this guide grows as the company does.

**Consistency does not mean every item looks the same. It means every item is
unmistakably Liyat.**
